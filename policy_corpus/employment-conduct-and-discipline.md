## Section 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy governs employee conduct expectations and the disciplinary process
that applies when those expectations are not met. It applies to all permanent
employees, fixed-term employees and contractors engaged directly by the company
in any jurisdiction in which the company operates. Where local employment law
imposes a stricter requirement than this policy, the local requirement prevails
and the People Operations team must be consulted before any action is taken.

## Section 2. Progressive Discipline

Except where Section 6 (Summary Dismissal) applies, disciplinary action follows
a progressive sequence: documented verbal warning, written warning, final
written warning, then termination. Each stage requires a documented meeting with
the employee, written notice of the specific conduct at issue, and a stated
period for improvement. A stage may only be skipped where the severity of the
conduct is documented in writing by the responsible manager and countersigned by
a People Operations partner.

An employee may not be moved to a later stage of the sequence for conduct that
was not raised at the preceding stage.

## Section 3. Investigation Requirements

No disciplinary action beyond a documented verbal warning may be taken before an
investigation has been completed. An investigation must include: a written
statement of the allegation provided to the employee at least three working days
before any investigatory meeting; an opportunity for the employee to respond;
collection of relevant evidence; and a written investigation report retained by
People Operations.

The employee has the right to be accompanied at any investigatory or
disciplinary meeting by a colleague or a union representative.

Investigations into alleged financial misconduct, discrimination, harassment or
retaliation must additionally be reviewed by Legal before any outcome is issued.

## Section 4. Approval Authority for High-Impact Actions

The following actions are high-impact and may not be executed on a manager's
authority alone. Each requires the approvals listed:

- Termination for cause: People Operations partner and Legal.
- Termination without cause / redundancy: People Operations partner, Legal and
  the relevant department head.
- Demotion or reduction in job level: People Operations partner and the
  department head.
- Reduction in base compensation: People Operations partner, department head and
  Finance.
- Suspension without pay: People Operations partner and Legal.
- Final written warning: People Operations partner.

Approval must be recorded in the employee's file before the action takes effect.
Verbal approval is not sufficient.

## Section 5. Suspension Pending Investigation

An employee may be suspended on full pay while an investigation is conducted
where their continued presence presents a risk to evidence, to colleagues, or to
company assets. Suspension on full pay is a neutral act and is not itself a
disciplinary sanction. It requires approval by a People Operations partner and
must be reviewed every ten working days.

Suspension without pay is a disciplinary sanction and may only follow a completed
investigation and the approvals in Section 4.

## Section 6. Summary Dismissal for Gross Misconduct

Summary dismissal -- termination without notice -- is permitted only for gross
misconduct. Gross misconduct includes theft, fraud, deliberate falsification of
records, violence, gross negligence causing material harm, and serious breach of
the Information Access and Security Policy.

Even where gross misconduct is alleged, summary dismissal requires a completed
investigation under Section 3 and the approvals in Section 4. The company does
not permit immediate termination on the strength of an allegation alone,
regardless of how serious the allegation is. The correct interim measure while
the allegation is investigated is suspension on full pay under Section 5.

## Section 7. Protected Characteristics and Retaliation

No disciplinary action may be taken, and no disciplinary process may be
initiated, on the basis of a protected characteristic, or in response to an
employee raising a good-faith complaint, making a protected disclosure, or
participating in an investigation. Any proposed action against an employee who
has raised a complaint within the preceding six months requires Legal review
before it proceeds, irrespective of the merits of the proposed action.

## Section 8. Performance Management

Performance concerns that do not amount to misconduct are handled under a
performance improvement plan rather than the disciplinary process. A performance
improvement plan runs for a minimum of sixty calendar days, states measurable
objectives, and includes scheduled check-ins. Termination for sustained
underperformance may only follow a completed performance improvement plan and
the approvals in Section 4.

## Section 9. Records and Retention

All disciplinary records, investigation reports and approval records are
retained by People Operations for the duration of employment plus seven years.
Employees may request a copy of their own disciplinary record. Warnings expire
and are disregarded for the purposes of the progressive sequence after twelve
months for a written warning and eighteen months for a final written warning.
